# Hyderabadi details that fit this method

> Researched 14 Nov 2026. Frame: the creator's recipe is fixed (chicken marinated in curd and ginger-garlic paste, fried till golden, made into the base/masala, rice added, pot on dum). Everything below is "what fits this method", not "what is most authentic".
>
> **How pages were read.** Pages were opened with a fetch tool that returns a summary from a small model, not raw text. Quoted phrases are as that tool reported them. Anything marked **(unverified)** comes from a search snippet or memory. Source kinds: *news/regional* = Hyderabad or Indian press; *Hyderabadi cook* = recipe by a named Hyderabad-linked cook; *recipe blog* = generic aggregator; *encyclopedia* = Wikipedia; *retail* = vendor page.

> **Art note (illustrated only, no photographs).** The shapes that matter for drawing are: tej patta as a long, olive, three-veined leaf (section 2), a cassia bark stick, a round heavy lidded pot with a dough-seal rim (section 5), and Irani chai in a small cup with a saucer (section 5). Check these against a Hyderabadi reviewer, since no reference images were opened.

## 1. Where the method sits

- Hyderabadi biryani has two named types. Kachchi: meat marinated overnight, layered raw with rice, sealed with dough. Pakki: meat marinated for less time and "semi-cooked before putting in a sealed vessel". Source: <https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/there-s-more-just-one-type-hyderabadi-biryani-and-here-s-how-different-they-are-61085> (news/regional). Wikipedia confirms kachchi (raw) and pakki (cooked) but only describes kachchi: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabadi_biryani> (encyclopedia).
- The creator's method (cook the marinated chicken first, rice on top, then dum) is **pakki**. A recipe blog states the pakki method as "cook the marinated meat with aromatics and make a gravy first", with par-cooked rice layered over it: <https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/chicken-biryani/> (recipe blog; its author is not shown as Hyderabadi on the page).
- It is a recognised home method. A Hyderabad-linked family recipe browns the chicken in ghee-fried onions and spices, simmers it with curd, layers rice on top and does a short dum (10 to 15 min): <https://gulfnews.com/food/how-hyderabadi-biryani-taught-me-not-all-of-indias-garam-masalas-are-the-same-1.1623155246948> (Gulf News; author says her husband is Hyderabadi, so a family recipe by marriage, not a Hyderabadi cook). Sanjeev Kapoor's Hyderabadi chicken biryani also cooks the marinated chicken first, in ghee for about 10 min, not "fried golden": <https://sanjeevkapoor.com/Recipe/hyderabadi-chicken-biryani-7601155> (celebrity chef recipe).
- **I found no source that names "fry the marinated chicken till golden, then rice" as its own style.** That frying detail is the creator's own, and the closest sourced label is "pakki".
- Honest short name for display: **"home-style pakki dum"** or in plain words **"cooked-masala dum biryani"**. Avoid "kachchi" and avoid "authentic Hyderabadi" as a claim. A safe caption: "Our family's way: chicken first, then rice, then dum."
- Contradiction with the earlier docs: `02-visual-direction.md` cites Wikipedia's kachchi description ("meat at the bottom of the degh under layered basmati") as the central prop. The layered-pot-with-dough-seal image still holds, but the chicken is already cooked in this method.

## 2. Whole spices

Prevalence is by what the recipes I opened used. The recipes are mostly blogs, so read "common" as "appears in most of the 6 to 8 recipes I opened", not a survey.

| Spice | English / romanised | Prevalence | Accidental bite |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shah jeera | Black cumin / caraway-like seed, *shahi zeera* ("imperial cumin"), also *kala zeera* | Common in the rice water or base (Swasthi, Kapoor, Wikipedia Hyderabadi list); Gulf News says "black cumin" | Smoky, earthy, small seed, harmless. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunium_persicum> (encyclopedia; flagged as needing citations). Not nigella (also called black cumin) |
| Green cardamom | *elaichi* | Near-universal | "Strong taste, aromatic, resinous", little is needed. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardamom> (encyclopedia). Home cooks say nearly every Indian has "accidentally bitten into a clove or cardamom pod"; one blog calls biting cardamom more "traumatic" than bay leaf: <https://myheartbeets.com/?p=11808> (recipe blog) |
| Black cardamom | big/greater cardamom, *bari elaichi* (name **unverified**) | Common but often "optional", one pod only | "Strong, camphor-like flavour, with a smoky character": <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_cardamom> (encyclopedia) |
| Clove | *laung* (**unverified** spelling) | Near-universal | Eugenol is "an anaesthetic": <https://www.compoundchem.com/2014/04/09/guarding-against-toothache-premature-ejaculation-the-chemistry-of-cloves/> (chemistry site). Numbing is real at oil strength but mild with one clove: <https://www.mashed.com/1239979/cloves-have-a-numbing-effect-but-you-probably-wont-notice> (snippet only, **unverified**). | Cinnamon or cassia | *dalchini* (**unverified**) | Near-universal | Cassia is "less delicate" than true cinnamon, thick and hard: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinnamomum_cassia> (encyclopedia). | Bay leaf | *tej patta*, which is *Cinnamomum tamala*, not European bay | Near-universal | Clove-like aroma with a peppery hint; looks different from Mediterranean bay (3 veins, larger): <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinnamomum_tamala> (encyclopedia). Texture is leathery; nothing sourced on the bite. Same blog calls it the least "traumatic" |
| Star anise | *chakri phool* (**unverified**; Hindi Wikipedia title is चक्रफूल) | **Contested.** Used by Swasthi (<https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/chicken-biryani/>) and Kapoor. A Gulf News piece says it "is a big no-no" in Hyderabadi biryani and not in the city's garam masala. Wikipedia's Hyderabadi page lists it | Anethole, anise-like sweetness: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_anise> (encyclopedia) |
| Mace | *javitri* (Wikipedia's Hyderabadi page lists "mace flower (javitri)") | Common, usually one blade | "Pungent fragrance and warm, slightly sweet" is the page's nutmeg description, mace itself not sourced (**unverified** for mace) |
| Black peppercorn | *kali mirch* | Common in the spice bag | Piperine heat, "a different kind of spiciness from capsaicin", with citrusy/woody notes: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_pepper>. Whole-berry chewing not described (inference: a short sharp heat) |
| Green chilli | *hari mirch* (**unverified**) | Common (Kapoor sautés it in ghee; slit chillies appear in many recipes) | Capsaicin heat. A real spike, building. Not sourced beyond general knowledge (**unverified**) |

**Wrong or unusual to show**
- **Star anise**: contested, so treat it as the rare find, not a default. A Hyderabadi viewer may call it wrong (Gulf News). Never show Japanese star anise (toxic, per Wikipedia), but nothing visual separates the two, so avoid text implying anything about it.
- **Bay leaf as "European bay"**: draw it as long, olive, three-veined tej patta, not a laurel leaf.
- **Cinnamon "quills" (Ceylon)**: the thick rough bark stick (cassia) is the better picture. (Inference.)
- Whole **cloves, cardamom and cinnamon sticks are normally left on the plate** by diners. Wikipedia and blogs confirm this habit only informally: <https://myheartbeets.com/?p=11808>. Showing a bite is fine, but the "wow, perfume shop" cardamom line is a design choice, not a documented reaction. Documented reactions are closer to "off-putting, spit or swallow".
- Peppercorn is the odd one out: small spices are often eaten (same blog), so the pepper egg is the most plausible.
- Black cumin/shah jeera belongs in the pot more than the star anise does, so consider it in the spice cabinet.

## 3. Signature ingredients and steps that fit this method

- **Birista (fried onion)**: "fried until golden-brown (birista)", used in the marinade and the layers. <https://www.sbs.com.au/food/recipe/hyderabadi-biryani-layered-chicken-and-rice-dum-biryani/tu7cceyyn> (SBS, recipe by Fuzz Ali; background not verified). Generally called a structural element: <https://diasporaco.com/blogs/journal/for-the-love-of-biryani> (snippet only, **unverified**). Spellings vary (berista); "birista" is what Hyderabad-linked recipes use. Etymology of the word itself: **could not verify**.
- **Saffron milk**: a few strands in 2 tbsp warm milk (<https://kalimirchbysmita.com/hyderabadi-mutton-biryani/>, recipe blog by Smita), poured over the top layer. Wikipedia lists saffron (zafran) for Hyderabadi biryani; the News Minute spells it "Zaffran".
- **Mint and coriander**: layered between rice and base, or in the marinade (Smita; Gulf News).
- **Ghee**: "generous portion of clarified butter" over the top layer (Smita); a blog says not to use oil at the end (**unverified**, search snippet).
- **Par-boiling**: wildly variable. 20% (2 min, Smita), about 70% (SBS), 90% (5 min, Swasthi pakki), 5 to 6 min (Kapoor). Pakki recipes cook the rice further because the dum is shorter. Spices go into the rice water, often in a muslin bag.
- **Layering order**: base (chicken masala) at the bottom, then rice, then fried onion, herbs, ghee, saffron milk. Repeat (SBS, Swasthi). Wikipedia's kachchi description says meat at the bottom.
- **Dough seal**: stiff flour dough ("flour, water and oil") pressed round the rim (SBS). Not every home cook uses it: Kapoor seals with aluminium foil. A "dum" seal made of dough forms a bread called a *pardah* ("veil") which is eaten: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dum_pukht> (encyclopedia).
- **Dum on a home stove**: an iron griddle/tawa under the pot is the standard trick. High heat 4 to 5 minutes first, then very low. Timings found: 35 min (Smita, mutton), 30 to 40 min (SBS), 18 to 20 min (Swasthi pakki), 15 to 20 min (Kapoor), 10 to 15 min (Gulf News family recipe). The pakki versions are shorter. Safe display: "a few minutes on high, then low and slow for about 20".
- **Resting**: 10 min (SBS, Swasthi, Wikibooks), 15 min (Smita). <https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Cookbook:Hyderabad_Biryani> (community cookbook).
- **Serving from the pot**: dismantle the seal, then use a wide spoon so each plate gets rice from the top and chicken from the bottom (Swasthi); "mix the medley from the bottom" (Wikibooks).
- The brainstorm doc has the dum as "seal, then wait with a trembling pot". Real home dum is nearly silent, with a quiet hiss and a faint rattle if any (**inference**). Do not show a rolling boil.

## 4. Accompaniments and after the meal

- **Mirchi ka salan and dahi ki chutney** are the standard pair: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabadi_biryani>. Mirchi ka salan is a chilli gravy on a peanut-sesame-coconut-tamarind base (snippet-level, **unverified** composition). Dahi ki chutney is curd with onion, coriander, mint and chilli, and "close to raita" (snippet, **unverified**).
- Wikipedia lists baghaar-e-baingan as a common side, and a salad of onion, carrot, cucumber and lemon wedges. Onion and lemon wedges are confirmed there; Swasthi adds kachumber and onion salad.
- **Raita** also appears (SBS, Smita, Wikibooks). The brainstorm doc's "raita bite" is fine. Add salan as a second option, since salan is the more Hyderabadi pair.
- Boiled egg halves appear with biryani in one recipe (Wikibooks). Optional.
- Dessert: double ka meetha (fried bread pudding) and khubani ka meetha (apricot dessert with cream or almonds), plus sheer korma, firni and faluda: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabadi_cuisine> (encyclopedia). Double ka meetha is described as a wedding staple (snippet, **unverified**).
- **Chai after the meal**: Irani chai is a Hyderabad staple (sources in section 5), but I found no source that says it follows biryani specifically. Treat "chai afterwards" as a plausible design choice, not a documented custom.

## 5. Vessels, serving ware, Irani-cafe details

**Vessels**
- Handi: round, heavy-bottomed pot for sealed slow cooking, "preferably a handi (clay pot)": <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dum_pukht>. A News Minute piece says kachchi uses a "handi sealed with dough" and Wikipedia says "degh" for the Hyderabadi biryani pot. Wikipedia and the News Minute use the two words for the same job.
- **Degh vs handi vs dekchi**: no reliable source separates them. A degh is sold as a large brass/aluminium pot (retail listings, snippet). **I found nothing defining dekchi.** Do not label the vessel with any of the three as a fact. Safer: draw a round, wide-mouthed, lidded heavy pot, and call it "the pot" or "handi".
- Home kitchens: sources that show home recipes use a heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven with a tawa underneath (SBS; embercookware blog snippet), or just a non-stick pan with foil (Kapoor). Clay is traditional but not what most home recipes use. Retail pages list copper, brass, aluminium, steel (<https://www.thehansindia.com/life-style/the-ultimate-guide-to-choosing-the-perfect-biryani-handi-939841>, sponsored; treat as marketing).
- Serving ware: Kapoor plates the biryani with salan drizzled on one side and fried chillies and onion rings on top. Steel plates are verified only for Irani-cafe snacks ("dull stainless steel plates", News Minute below). Banana leaf: **not found** in Hyderabadi sources; avoid it.

## 7. Clichés and inaccuracies a Hyderabadi viewer might object to

- Calling this "kachchi" or "the authentic" method. See section 1.
- Potatoes in the pot. Source: a Hyderabadi opinion piece says potatoes in biryani are "an abomination" for many Hyderabadis (snippet from <https://theprint.in/opinion/forget-hyderabad-hype-bengaluru-biryani-scene-has-gone-unnoticed/2412964/>, **unverified**). Potato is the Kolkata signature.
- Star anise as a default spice (section 2).
- A tall glass for chai, a banana leaf, a chequered floor, or a "royal Nizam" bowl-and-scimitar look as the only frame. The existing doc already warns against "royal Nizam" shorthand.
- Unspecific "Mughal" Taj Mahal imagery (existing doc).
- Showing salan or dahi chutney as optional garnish. They are part of the standard pairing.
- Calling it spicy-hot without nuance. Opinions split (the same opinion piece calls Hyderabadi biryani "over the top too spicy"; restaurant reviewers say full of masala, not hot).
- Using Hindi-style spellings that Hyderabadi Urdu speakers would change, or adding Urdu/Telugu text without proofreading.
- The bucket-biryani format is a modern novelty; "Hyderabadis are divided" (<https://telanganatoday.com/hyderabads-biryani-game-from-pot-to-bucket-a-delicious-odyssey>). Don't use it as the vessel.
